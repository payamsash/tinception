Going to your second question: my answer is **yes — but almost none of the subtypes we publish are among them.**

The subtype distinctions have one thing in common: they were defined in the clinic first, and *then* tested for biology.

we have examples like **Pulsatile tinnitus** which is genuinely a different condition or **Bilateral versus one-sided tinnitus** which they differ in how strongly they're inherited And **constant versus occasional tinnitus** actually occasional tinnitus looks like no tinnitus at all.

but what I did during my PHD and also the 2 talks that I will have in TRI are exactly about tinnitus subtyping but from 2 completely different perspectives. which is can we find tinnuts subtypes by looking at the brain activity or brain anatomy?

I want to briefly explain the two study and what we found?

The first one is an ongoing study within the TIDE consortium, which is about tinnitus identification and finding a biomarker for tinnitus based on the brain activity. what we found there is that we can reliabily classify tinnitus from non-tinnitus subjects solely based on their brain activity but when we pushed it bit more we noticed that there are group of tinnitus people that our models are not capable of separating them from controls and they are following a different path in the brain to generate tinnitus which is totally unseen to our models. which is clear sign to having subtypes within the tinnitus group.


In the other study we looked at brain anatomy in a large scale to find differences between tinnitis and non-tinnitus subjects if there is any and also within the tinnitus group to see what explains more the differences between tinnius people. what we found there is very interesting: we saw that there are very small regions with amygdalar, hippocampus and thalamic nuclie which their morphology clearly separate the tinnitus subjects into two distinct subtypes. which actually its a hint that there are 2 separate different paths in the brain for phantom sound generation.

so in summery what I can conclude is that we have high heterogeneity with tinnitus subjects but we are able to summerize this heterogeneity into subtypes but there are questions we still dont knwo the answers for example: is there a subtype that can predict the treatment response, or how someone does over next two years. or are these subtypes a gradient rather than a set of boxes?
